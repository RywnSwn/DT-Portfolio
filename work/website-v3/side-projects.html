Trash Holder Version 2 in progress

The problem

My nose runs and I sneeze all the time, and it has been like that my whole life. Used tissues end up in a pile next to me on the desk, so I wanted somewhere right at my desk to put them. This grew out of the "tissue holder under table" idea from the original brainstorm.

Why a clamp

  • Tape is easy to move, but it gets weak over time.
  • Screwing into the desk is too permanent.
  • A clamp holds strong and can still move to another desk.

It uses a microphone-style twist clamp, so I did not need to measure the desk. You just screw it tight onto the edge.

Version 1

One clamp with a ring on an arm. The plastic trash bag goes through the ring, which has a hole in the middle. The inner ring has small supports so it stays connected, and a screw in the clamp tightens it onto the desk. I got about halfway in Fusion 360 and then switched to Version 2.

Version 2

Two clamps, one on each side, with a tub sitting in the middle. I changed to this because it is modular: the clamps can adjust to different tables without redesigning everything. The tub replaces the trash bag.

Making

Modeled in Fusion 360, about a day per part. This is a side project, so I work on it when I am a bit burnt out on the chess board. One part is printed so far (below) and it came out fine. The tub is modeled and printing next. Still to print: the second clamp and 2 screws.

Testing

Not tested yet. I do not have the screw yet, so the printed part has not been fitted to the clamp, and nobody has used it.

Next steps

  1. Print the tub
  2. Print the second clamp and 2 screws
  3. Assemble it and fit it to my desk
  4. Use it for a while and note what works and what does not
  5. Fix anything that comes up

Bear Fix Done

A classmate had a toy model of a bear that was giving them trouble. I fixed the file in Blender and gave it back.

How I did it

  • Removed the stray shape connected to the hand
  • Remeshed it in Blender because I thought it would look better a little less poly
  • Tried subdividing afterwards, but it looked terrible
  • Could have sculpted it smooth instead, but that was a lot of work for a favour, so I skipped it

Next time

Subdivide, sculpt it smooth, then decimate again to bring the poly count back down.
